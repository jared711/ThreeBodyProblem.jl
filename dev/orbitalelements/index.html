Overview

The classical Keplerian orbital elements are $[a, e, i Ω, ω, ν]$. While these are the most well-known and commonly used in the astrodynamics community, they are not the only way to describe an orbit. We are also working on including two-line elements and Delaunay variables.

Functions

ThreeBodyProblem.cart2oe — Function
cart2oe(rv::Array, μ; ang_unit::Symbol=:deg)

Convert cartesian coordinates [km;km/s] in the ECI frame to orbital elements (a, e, i, Ω, ω, ν, Π, u, l) and specific orbital energy ℰ given gravitational parameter μ. Angles that are undefined for the orbit (e.g. Ω, ω and ν of a circular equatorial orbit) are NaN.

See also: oe2cart

Examples

julia> cart2oe([1,0,0,0,1,0], 1, ang_unit=:deg)
(1.0, 0.0, 0.0, NaN, NaN, NaN, NaN, NaN, 0.0, -0.5)
